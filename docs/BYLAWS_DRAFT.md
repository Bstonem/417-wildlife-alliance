## BYLAWS OF 417 WILDLIFE ALLIANCE

*A Missouri Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation*

### ARTICLE I — Name, Purpose, and Offices

**1.1 Name.** The name of the corporation is 417 Wildlife Alliance (the "Corporation").

**1.2 Purpose.** The Corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, educational, and scientific purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including supporting licensed and permitted wildlife rehabilitation, educating the public on safe and lawful wildlife response, reducing preventable wildlife harm, and funding supplies, transport, emergency care, and other charitable support for wildlife rehabilitation in Missouri and surrounding regions, as further described in the Articles of Incorporation.

**1.3 Principal Office.** The Corporation's principal office is located at `[ADDRESS]`, or at such other place as the Board of Directors may designate.

**1.4 Registered Agent.** The Corporation's registered agent and registered office in Missouri are `[REGISTERED AGENT NAME AND ADDRESS]`, as may be updated from time to time by Board resolution and filing with the Missouri Secretary of State.

### ARTICLE II — No Members

**2.1 No Statutory Members.** The Corporation has no members with voting rights under Missouri nonprofit corporation law. All corporate powers are exercised by, and the activities of the Corporation are managed by or under the direction of, the Board of Directors. References elsewhere in these bylaws to "members" of a committee mean participants on that committee, not statutory members of the Corporation.

### ARTICLE III — Board of Directors

**3.1 General Powers.** The business and affairs of the Corporation are managed by its Board of Directors.

**3.2 Number and Qualifications.** The Board consists of not fewer than five (5) and not more than `[MAXIMUM NUMBER]` directors, the exact number to be set by Board resolution from time to time. Directors need not be Missouri residents. The Board should include at least `[NUMBER]` directors who are not related to one another and not compensated employees of the Corporation, to support donor and grantor trust.

**3.3 Election and Term.** Directors are elected by a majority vote of the directors then in office. Each director serves a term of `[ONE/TWO/THREE]` year(s) or until a successor is elected, and may be re-elected for successive terms without limit unless the Board later adopts term limits.

**3.4 Initial Directors.** The initial Board consists of the incorporator(s) and any additional directors named in the organizational resolutions adopted at the first Board meeting.

**3.5 Resignation.** A director may resign at any time by written notice to the President/Chair or Secretary. A resignation is effective when received unless a later date is specified.

**3.6 Removal.** A director may be removed, with or without cause, by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the remaining directors then in office, at a meeting called for that purpose with notice stating that removal will be considered.

**3.7 Vacancies.** A vacancy on the Board, however caused, may be filled by a majority vote of the remaining directors, even if fewer than a quorum. A director elected to fill a vacancy serves the remainder of the unexpired term.

**3.8 Compensation.** Directors serve without compensation for their service as directors, but may be reimbursed for reasonable, documented expenses incurred on the Corporation's behalf, subject to the Corporation's expense reimbursement policy. Nothing in this section prevents a director from being reasonably compensated for services rendered to the Corporation in a capacity other than as a director (for example, as a paid staff member), subject to Article VII (Conflict of Interest) and board approval with the interested director recused.

**3.9 Regular Meetings.** The Board holds at least `[NUMBER, e.g., FOUR]` regular meetings per year, at times and places (including by remote/virtual means) set by the Board.

**3.10 Annual Meeting.** The Board holds an annual meeting each `[MONTH]` for the election of officers, review of the prior year's activities and finances, and other business.

**3.11 Special Meetings.** Special meetings may be called by the President/Chair, the Treasurer, or any two directors, on at least `[NUMBER, e.g., FIVE]` days' notice stating the purpose(s) of the meeting.

**3.12 Notice.** Notice of any meeting may be given by mail, email, or other written or electronic means reasonably likely to reach each director, and may be waived in writing (including by attendance without objection).

**3.13 Quorum.** A majority of the directors then in office constitutes a quorum for the transaction of business.

**3.14 Voting.** Except as otherwise required by law, the Articles of Incorporation, or these bylaws (for example, Section 3.6 on removal and Article XI on amendments), each director has one vote, and the act of a majority of directors present at a meeting at which a quorum is present is the act of the Board.

**3.15 Remote Participation.** Directors may participate in a meeting by conference call, video conference, or similar means by which all participants can hear one another; participation in this manner constitutes presence in person.

**3.16 Action Without a Meeting.** The Board may act without a meeting if all directors consent in writing (including by email), and the consents are filed with the corporate records.

**3.17 Founder Recognition and Founder Director Seats.**

(a) *Recognition.* Matthew Blystone and KJ Hosman are recognized as the Founders of the Corporation (each, a "Founder") in acknowledgment of their roles in conceiving, organizing, and launching 417 Wildlife Alliance. This recognition may be reflected in the Corporation's public materials, corporate records, and communications, and each Founder may describe their role accordingly.

(b) *Founder Director Seats.* Beginning on the date the Missouri Secretary of State accepts the Corporation's Articles of Incorporation and continuing until the date three (3) years after the Internal Revenue Service issues the Corporation's Section 501(c)(3) determination letter (the "Founder Term"), each Founder is entitled to serve as a director of the Corporation without standing for the periodic re-election otherwise required by Section 3.3, provided that Founder remains willing and able to serve. If the Corporation has not received its determination letter within five (5) years after Missouri accepts the Articles of Incorporation, the Founder Term instead ends three (3) years after that fifth anniversary. This entitlement does not exempt either Founder from Section 3.5 (Resignation) or Section 3.6 (Removal), applies independently to each Founder (the resignation, removal, or ineligibility of one Founder does not affect the other's seat), and does not limit the Board's ability to amend or repeal this Section 3.17 under Article XI.

(c) *After the Founder Term.* After the Founder Term ends, a Founder may continue to serve as a director on the same basis as any other director, subject to the standard election process in Section 3.3.

(d) *Officer and Staff Roles Not Guaranteed.* This Section does not guarantee either Founder any specific officer title (Article IV) or staff position. Those remain subject to the Board's ordinary election, hiring, and compensation-setting processes, including the conflict-of-interest recusal required by Article VII whenever the Board considers compensating a Founder.

### ARTICLE IV — Officers

**4.1 Officers.** The officers of the Corporation are a President (or Chair), a Secretary, and a Treasurer, and may include a Vice President and other officers the Board creates. One person may hold more than one office, except that the same person should not simultaneously serve as both President/Chair and Treasurer, to support separation of financial duties.

**4.2 Election and Term.** Officers are elected annually by the Board at the annual meeting and serve a one-year term or until a successor is elected, and may be re-elected.

**4.3 President/Chair.** The President (or Chair) presides at Board meetings, is the Corporation's chief governance officer, and performs the duties customary to that office, subject to Board direction.

**4.4 Vice President.** The Vice President, if any, performs the President's duties in the President's absence or incapacity.

**4.5 Secretary.** The Secretary ensures that notices are given, keeps minutes of Board meetings, is custodian of the corporate records, and ensures required filings and records are maintained.

**4.6 Treasurer.** The Treasurer oversees the Corporation's finances, ensures accurate financial records are kept, presents financial reports to the Board at each regular meeting, and coordinates with any external bookkeeper, accountant, or auditor. The Treasurer does not have sole, unilateral authority to move Corporation funds; disbursements above `[THRESHOLD, e.g., $500]` require a second authorized signer or Board approval, per the Corporation's financial controls.

**4.7 Removal and Vacancies.** An officer may be removed, with or without cause, by a majority vote of the Board. A vacancy in any office may be filled by the Board for the unexpired term.

### ARTICLE V — Committees

**5.1 Standing and Ad Hoc Committees.** The Board may establish standing or ad hoc committees (for example, a Program Committee, a Fundraising/Partnerships Committee, or a Finance/Audit Committee) and appoint directors, staff, or outside volunteers to serve on them. Non-directors may serve on committees but may not vote on matters reserved to the Board by law.

**5.2 Authority.** No committee may take action that binds the Corporation unless the Board has delegated that specific authority by resolution, and a committee may never (a) amend the Articles of Incorporation or these bylaws, (b) approve dissolution or merger, or (c) take any other action reserved to the full Board under Missouri law.

### ARTICLE VI — Program Limitations

**6.1 Launch Boundary.** Consistent with the Corporation's Articles of Incorporation, the Corporation will not directly possess, transport, hold, treat, or rehabilitate wildlife unless and until it has obtained all required state and federal permits and approvals for that activity. Until then, the Corporation's programs are limited to public education, found-animal routing to licensed/permitted third parties, funding and grants/reimbursements to licensed/permitted rehabilitators and organizations, directory/referral services, and partner/sponsor/education programs.

**6.2 Review.** The Board will review this boundary at least annually and may amend this Article if the Corporation obtains the permits, facilities, insurance, and qualified personnel needed to responsibly expand its programs.

### ARTICLE VII — Conflict of Interest

**7.1 Policy.** The Corporation will maintain a separate, Board-adopted Conflict of Interest Policy consistent with the sample policy in the instructions to IRS Form 1023. This Article summarizes its core requirements; the full policy governs in case of any inconsistency.

**7.2 Disclosure.** Each director and officer must disclose any financial interest, or a close relative's financial interest, in any proposed transaction or arrangement with the Corporation, including grants, reimbursements, contracts, or compensation.

**7.3 Recusal.** An interested director or officer must not participate in the discussion, deliberation, or vote on the matter (other than to answer questions), and must not be present for the vote.

**7.4 Documentation.** The minutes must reflect the disclosure, the recusal, and the basis for the Board's decision, including any comparability data used to confirm the arrangement was fair and reasonable to the Corporation.

**7.5 Annual Statements.** Each director and officer must complete an annual conflict-of-interest disclosure statement.

### ARTICLE VIII — Indemnification

**8.1 Indemnification.** To the fullest extent permitted by Missouri law, the Corporation will indemnify any person made or threatened to be made a party to a proceeding because that person is or was a director, officer, employee, or agent of the Corporation, against judgments, penalties, fines, settlements, and reasonable expenses (including attorney's fees) actually incurred, provided the person acted in good faith and in a manner reasonably believed to be in the Corporation's best interests.

**8.2 Insurance.** The Board may purchase and maintain director and officer liability insurance, general liability insurance, and any other insurance it considers appropriate.

### ARTICLE IX — Fiscal Year and Financial Controls

**9.1 Fiscal Year.** The Corporation's fiscal year is the calendar year, January 1 through December 31, unless the Board changes it on the advice of its accountant.

**9.2 Budget.** The Board adopts an annual budget before or near the start of each fiscal year.

**9.3 Financial Reports.** The Treasurer presents a financial report, including income, expenses, and fund balances, at each regular Board meeting.

**9.4 Bank Accounts.** All Corporation funds are held in accounts in the Corporation's name at institutions approved by Board resolution. Personal accounts must never be used to receive or hold donations or other Corporation funds.

**9.5 Signing Authority.** Checks, drafts, and other orders for payment of money, and contracts obligating the Corporation, are signed by the officer(s) or agent(s) the Board authorizes by resolution, consistent with the dual-authorization threshold in Section 4.6.

**9.6 Restricted and Designated Funds.** The Corporation will not represent a gift as restricted to a specific use unless it has an accounting system capable of tracking and honoring that restriction, consistent with the Corporation's gift acceptance and fund restriction policies.

**9.7 Annual Review.** The Board arranges an annual financial review, audit, or compilation appropriate to the Corporation's size, and reviews the results.

### ARTICLE X — Books, Records, and Compliance

**10.1 Records.** The Corporation keeps correct and complete minutes of Board and committee proceedings, adequate financial records, and a record of directors and officers, consistent with its document retention policy.

**10.2 Annual Filings.** The Corporation timely files its Missouri Secretary of State annual report, its IRS annual return (Form 990-N, 990-EZ, or 990, as applicable), and any required Missouri Attorney General charitable registration renewal.

**10.3 Public Inspection.** The Corporation makes available for public inspection the documents required by law, including its IRS Form 1023 application, determination letter, and the three most recent Forms 990, once filed.

### ARTICLE XI — Amendments

**11.1 Amendment.** These bylaws may be amended, in whole or in part, by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the directors then in office, at a meeting for which notice of the proposed amendment was given, or by unanimous written consent under Section 3.16.

### ARTICLE XII — Dissolution

**12.1 Dissolution.** Upon dissolution of the Corporation, the Board will, after paying or providing for the Corporation's debts and liabilities, distribute all remaining assets exclusively to one or more organizations that are themselves exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code and organized for charitable, educational, or scientific purposes consistent with the Corporation's mission, as the Board determines, subject to approval by a court of competent jurisdiction if required by Missouri law. No asset will be distributed to any director, officer, or private individual.

### ARTICLE XIII — Adoption

These bylaws were adopted by the Board of Directors of 417 Wildlife Alliance on `[DATE]`.

## Notes for the board (delete before adoption)

- Fill in every bracketed item, then have a Missouri nonprofit attorney review the whole document before adoption — this draft has not been reviewed by counsel.
- Article II (no members) matches the "no statutory members" recommendation in `docs/BUSINESS_PLAN.md` Section 7, Step 1. If the board later wants a voting membership structure, this article (and several director-election provisions) would need to be rewritten.
- Article VI (Program Limitations) encodes the "fund/route/educate, not directly rehabilitate" launch boundary from the business plan. Putting it in the bylaws (rather than just a board resolution) makes it easy to enforce and easy to formally revisit later.
- Section 4.6's dual-signer threshold and Section 9.6's restricted-fund language both track the "no donor-selectable fund categories until accounting controls exist" position already recorded in `docs/BUSINESS_PLAN.md`.
- This draft does not include a separate Conflict of Interest Policy, Gift Acceptance Policy, Whistleblower Policy, or the other documents listed in `docs/BUSINESS_PLAN.md` Section 8 — Article VII only summarizes the conflict-of-interest requirements. Those still need to be drafted separately (the IRS Form 1023 instructions include a usable sample conflict-of-interest policy).
- **Section 3.17 (Founder Recognition and Founder Director Seats)** guarantees Matthew Blystone and KJ Hosman each a board seat, without standing for re-election, from Missouri accepting the Articles of Incorporation through 3 years after the IRS issues the Corporation's 501(c)(3) determination letter, plus formal recognition as Founders. It intentionally stops short of permanent or irrevocable seats: a time-boxed, amendable guarantee is much more defensible to the IRS and to grantmakers than open-ended founder control, which can read as private benefit/inurement and slow down or complicate Form 1023 review. A few things worth doing alongside this:
  - **Board size matters more now that two seats are guaranteed — but a 5-director board only fixes majority control, not supermajority blocking.** At 5 directors, the two Founders are 2 of 5 (40%), so they cannot pass ordinary business on their own by simple majority — the 3 independent directors control anything that only needs a plain majority. But 2/3 of 5 rounds up to 4 votes, and a 40% bloc is comfortably above the 1/3 needed to block any 2/3-supermajority vote — meaning the Founders can, by voting together, always block a for-cause removal under Section 3.6 or a bylaws amendment under Article XI (including one repealing Section 3.17 itself), no matter what the 3 independent directors want. This was a deliberate choice, not an oversight: it keeps a future board from removing a Founder or stripping Section 3.17 without at least one Founder's cooperation, which directly serves the durable-influence goal behind this Section. The tradeoff is independence optics — a reviewer who does this math will see that the Founders retain real protection against the board's own supermajority checks. Disclose this dynamic in the Form 1023 governance narrative rather than leaving it to be discovered, for the same reason as the seat itself. If priorities change later, eliminating this blocking power entirely would require growing the board to at least 7 (2 Founders + 5 independents), which lets independents reach 2/3 without any Founder vote — but doing that also makes it correspondingly easier for a future board to remove a Founder or repeal Section 3.17 over both Founders' objections.
  - When you file Form 1023, disclose this provision directly in the governance narrative rather than leaving it to be discovered — reviewers respond much better to disclosed, time-limited arrangements than to ones that look hidden.
  - Because the end date is tied to whenever the IRS happens to issue the determination letter, the term's actual length is somewhat open-ended if that review drags on. The 5-year backstop in the second sentence caps that risk: if the letter still hasn't arrived 5 years after Missouri formation, the term is fixed at ending 3 years after that 5-year mark, regardless of what the IRS does after that.
  - The seats only guarantee board *membership*, not an officer title (President/Chair) or paid role (Executive Director) — those still go through the Board's normal election/hiring process each cycle. If ongoing operational influence matters as much as the board seat, plan to be nominated for one of those roles too, since that's where the actual day-to-day direction-setting happens.
  - If Matthew Blystone and KJ Hosman are related, married, domestic partners, or business partners outside the Corporation, note that under Section 4.6 they still should not simultaneously hold the President/Chair and Treasurer roles together, and any transaction between the Corporation and either of them still requires the Article VII conflict-of-interest recusal process.
