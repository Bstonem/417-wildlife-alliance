# 417 Wildlife Alliance: Business Plan Summary

Status: Draft for founder/partner review
Date: 2026-08-22
Primary region: Southwest Missouri / 417 area

This is a condensed, strategy-focused version of the full plan for sharing with a partner. It leaves out the detailed nonprofit-formation and legal-compliance steps (IRS forms, Missouri filing procedures, permit rules, statute citations) — those live in [`docs/BUSINESS_PLAN.md`](BUSINESS_PLAN.md) for founder/legal reference.

## 1. Executive Summary

417 Wildlife Alliance is launching as a Missouri nonprofit that supports licensed and permitted wildlife rehabilitators, connects the public to safe wildlife guidance, and builds a transparent local funding network for injured, orphaned, displaced, and at-risk wildlife in the 417 region.

The launch model deliberately does not include direct wildlife rehabilitation (which would require state/federal permits, facilities, and vet support). Instead:

1. Public education and found-animal routing.
2. A rehabber support fund for supplies, transport, emergency needs, and approved micro-grants or reimbursements.
3. A privacy-aware directory of licensed/permitted rehabbers and organizations.
4. Partner and sponsor programs.
5. A future Wildlife Compassionate Company program for tree care and other wildlife-adjacent businesses.

The website already implements this: found-animal intake, donation checkout, rehabber directory and self-service accounts, partner applications, certified-company pages, stories, FAQ, and admin tools. The main remaining work is not more site features — it's legal formation, donation readiness, and operating discipline.

## 2. Mission, Vision, And Positioning

**Mission:** Help people help wildlife by routing found animals to appropriate licensed or permitted care, funding the people and organizations doing rehabilitation work, and educating the community to prevent avoidable wildlife harm.

**Vision:** Southwest Missouri becomes a place where people know what to do when wildlife is in trouble, rehabbers have more reliable support, and local businesses understand how to reduce displacement and injury before it happens.

**Positioning:** The regional wildlife-help hub and rehabber support fund for the 417 area.

**Launch promise:** Helping people help wildlife, and helping rehabbers keep saying yes.

## 3. Programs

1. **Found-Animal Help And Routing** — Give the public calm, safe next steps and route cases to licensed/permitted care. The site never implies the public should keep, feed, or treat wildlife itself.
2. **Wildlife Rehabilitation Support Fund** — Make small, practical rehab expenses (formula, supplies, transport, emergency care, micro-grants/reimbursements) fundable and trackable, with written approval criteria and disbursement records.
3. **Rehabber Directory** — Help residents find help without exposing private rehabber information or overwhelming rehabbers; publish only consented contact details with "last verified" dates.
4. **Education And Prevention** — Baby-animal decision trees, "don't feed, call first" guidance, seasonal alerts, and impact stories to reduce unsafe public handling.
5. **Partners, Sponsors, And Certified Companies** — Build revenue and prevention partnerships with vets, tree care, landscaping, pest control, and local businesses. The Wildlife Compassionate Company program is education/recognition, not a guarantee or legal accreditation.

## 4. Revenue Model

**Revenue streams:** individual donations, monthly donors, founding sponsors, business partner/sponsor packages, certified-company fees (once criteria are approved), grants, events/fundraisers, merch or print-on-demand proceeds.

**Note on donations:** the donate page currently sends every gift to a single "General Fund" — there is no donor-selectable fund category on the site today. Reintroducing donor-directed categories should wait until the board has accounting controls that can actually track and honor restricted funds.

## 5. The Website As The Operating System

The website isn't a brochure — it's meant to do five jobs:

1. Help someone who found an animal make a safer next move.
2. Convert donors into one-time and monthly supporters.
3. Build trust with transparent fund priorities and impact reporting.
4. Recruit rehabbers, volunteers, partners, sponsors, and certified-company applicants.
5. Give admins structured records for grants, accounting, and follow-up.

Before taking real donations, the site still needs: an EIN field and updated tax-status language once available, a donation receipt email workflow, a refund policy and privacy/terms pages, and an admin export for donations and receipt status.

## 6. Marketing And Audiences

**Audiences:** people who found animals, wildlife rehabilitators and nonprofits, local donors, tree care/landscaping/pest control/veterinary/wellness businesses, community groups and schools, grantmakers.

**Launch campaigns:** Founding 100 monthly donors; baby-season formula fund; sponsor-a-rehabber-supply-shelf; wildlife-aware tree care pilot; transport/emergency support fund; impact story series.

**Trust signals:** board names and roles, tax status and EIN, a clear informational list of what gifts fund, annual impact report, directory verification dates, named sponsors, written grant/reimbursement criteria, public privacy and safety policies.

## 7. Key Metrics

- **Program:** found-animal submissions, cases routed, average first response time, rehabbers listed, counties/species served.
- **Fund:** one-time and monthly donations, donor retention, total raised vs. disbursed, rehabbers/organizations supported.
- **Partners:** sponsor leads and approvals, certified-company applicants, renewals.
- **Website:** donate conversion rate, found-animal form completion rate, directory searches, admin response time.
